**play/chase called out** per the frozen-world addendum §1 — it is the

**The meow group is 0.0000, and that is not a rounding artefact — the
clone never predicts a meow at all.** Dataset v3 carries 3,892 channel
decisions in 1.9M (0.2%), spread over three of six kinds, and label
smoothing at ε = 0.05 leaves no gradient strong enough to overcome a
500:1 imbalance. Combined with the dataset's empty `GroomKitty` and
deliberate-`Purr` classes, this means **PPO must discover the channel by
exploration, not refine it by fine-tuning** — the first generation here
to face that, because exp-002 inherited channel behaviour from s6. It
is also why the prereg drops H4 rather than carrying it: there is no
predecessor to preserve, and no floor to hold.

### The 20-epoch default is wrong for this dataset

The first run stopped at the script's default cap of 20 epochs with val
top-1 still rising monotonically (0.5763 → 0.7362, no plateau in sight)
— an under-trained clone that would have looked like a finished one.
§5 registers a **plateau stop and no epoch limit**, so the cap was the
script's, not the protocol's; re-running to 120 with patience 3 reached
the actual plateau at 61. exp-001 hit the same thing and used
`--epochs 300`. Anyone re-running this must pass `--epochs` high enough
to let the plateau rule decide.

## Critics

One per γ, never shared across γ (§5). States padded to the 5-kitty
layout; targets censored to ≥ 1500 realized ticks; normalizer frozen and
recorded.

| γ | target mean / std | best epoch | val MSE | **val EV** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.995 | 173.78 / 3.14 | 7 | 0.1661 | **0.8162** |
| 0.998 | 432.81 / 8.39 | 6 | 0.2725 | **0.6965** |

292,545 train / 97,515 val states each.

**Both are below exp-002's (0.891 / 0.845), and γ=0.998 markedly so.**
Explained variance is the make-or-break critic diagnostic per
F-003/F-005/F-006, so this is worth naming rather than burying. Two
candidate causes, neither verified here: v3's family is materially more
varied than v2's (four geometries including 20×20, water stratified
across the lake threshold, rosters 3–5), which makes returns harder to
predict from state; and v3 has no policy-generated rollouts, so the
state distribution is narrower in a different way. PPO trains the critic
further, so this is a starting position, not a verdict — but if value
loss misbehaves early in training, this table is the first place to look.

| roster | subject welfare | Δ vs `needs_driven` | worst distress age |
|---|---|---|---|
| AllSubject | 0.7920 | **−0.1126** | 818 |
| Mixed | 0.8675 | *(kitty-eval pairs baselines for AllSubject only)* | 573 |

**Welfare bounds do not pass** — unresolved-distress ages of 305–818
ticks against a limit of 150, in every seed. **This is expected and
precedented**: exp-001's clone, at a slightly higher top-1 of 0.8016,
failed the same way in every seed and was recorded as "expected for a
greedy 0.80-accuracy clone — the 20% mistaken decisions compound over
20k ticks, and greedy seating discards the calibrated uncertainty the
smoothed clone actually learned."

For scale: uniform-random floor ≈ 0.53–0.57, `needs_driven` = 0.9046 on
this engine. The clone lands at 0.79 AllSubject / 0.87 Mixed — most of
the way up from the floor, well short of the demonstrator it imitates.
exp-001's clone was −0.1513 AllSubject; this one is **−0.1126**, i.e.
somewhat closer to its demonstrator despite a lower top-1, on a harder
family.

**None of this is gated.** §9.2 and §9.3 apply to candidates after PPO,
not to the initialization. The clone's job is to be a better starting
point than random, to load, and to have entropy left to explore with.
It is, it does, and it has.
