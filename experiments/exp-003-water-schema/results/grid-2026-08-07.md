**Grooming-on-water, reported and deliberately not gated** (F-016), is
the sharpest evidence the observation bit did its job: 0.12–0.75%
against a scripted 0.51%. F-016 established that raising the dial makes
*scripted* cats groom on water more, because being wet raises the Bath
need and a needs-driven cat bathes where it stands. A policy that can
see it is wet does not do that. Splitting this channel out rather than
gating it turned it from a confound into the cleanest single readout of
the generation.

**Two populations.** Six candidates show *only* eat, in trace amounts —
3 to 212 threshold-ticks across 100,000 cat-ticks — with peak bath at
64–75, comfortably below distress. Three (A0-s2, A1-s1, A0-s3) show
broad multi-need saturation, which is what F-010 catatonia looks like.

**Bath is a symptom, never a cause.** It reaches 100 only inside the
collapses, because a cat that has stopped functioning has stopped
grooming. Water cannot cause bath distress by construction: the wet-fur
charge applies only below `bath_gain_ceiling` (60) and validation forces
`ceiling + gain × max_ratio < safeguard (75)`, while distress fires at
90.

### Two mechanisms proposed and refuted (recorded, because they were wrong)

1. **"The 3.5/60 dial pushes Bath toward distress."** False, and
   structurally so — see above. The inequality that makes the safeguard
   unreachable by water makes distress unreachable too.
2. **"The distress is cuddle-driven, and the policies never learned to
   meow for help."** An artifact of an underpowered probe: three
   candidates × three seeds on seeds disjoint from the eval found 37
   threshold-ticks, 68% of them cuddle. On the actual worst seeds cuddle
   is 0.7% pooled and **zero for six of nine candidates**. The story was
   appealing and wrong.

No third mechanism is offered. What is measured is that six candidates
fail on brief eat-timing lapses and three fail by collapsing.

## The world is not the explanation

Testing whether exp-003's roster worlds are simply harsher — same
engine, same policies, only the world differs (30 seeds × 20k):

| world | zero | median | worst |
|---|---|---|---|
| exp-002's roster-3 (22×22, water 7–8) | 2/9 | 33.0 | 14,303 |
| exp-003's roster-3 (20×20, water 3, lakeless) | 4/9 | 11.0 | 10,897 |
| exp-002's roster-5 (24×24, water 8–9) | 2/9 | 41.0 | 124 |
| exp-003's roster-5 (24×24, water 7) | 0/9 | 71.0 | 14,824 |

exp-003's candidates score 2/9 at zero on exp-002's **own** roster
worlds, where exp-002's candidates scored 14/22 and 12/22. If anything
they do *better* on exp-003's harsher roster-3 world. **World shape is
ruled out.**

That leaves the engine or the policies, and the engine route cannot be
tested: schema-1 artifacts do not load here (exit 1), which is the
retirement recorded in the design-inputs register. One weak signal in
the engine's favour: the `needs_driven` baseline itself fell from the
registered 0.906–0.908 to 0.9039–0.9054 on the same served world, so
the current world is measurably harder for a scripted cat — just not
enough to trip a distress event. **The question stays open.**

## Not discovered: the channel

Greedy `meow/1k` is 0.01–0.41 across all nine. The BC clone predicts
meows at 0.0000 accuracy, dataset v3 carries 3,892 channel decisions in
1.9M (0.2%) over three of six kinds, and no schema-2 policy existed to
seed the rest. **H4 was dropped from this prereg for exactly this
reason** — no predecessor to preserve — and the outcome confirms the
reasoning: PPO did not discover the channel from exploration alone in
20M ticks.
