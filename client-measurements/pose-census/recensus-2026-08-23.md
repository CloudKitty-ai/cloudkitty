## The client is not amplifying

| | |
| --- | --- |
| engine hunt actions | `play` 14.1% + `chase` 12.0% = **26.1%** of cat-ticks |
| client draws `pouncing` | **24.0%** |

Fewer pounces drawn than hunt actions applied — far chases correctly fall
through to walking. Three further checks, all clear:

- **The unresolved-quarry fallback fired zero times.** All 210 chase ticks
  resolved their target, so the one client rule that could inflate the
  count ("an unknown quarry keeps the pounce") contributed nothing.
- **The gate is doing its job.** Median quarry distance 2 tiles; 81.9% of
  resolved chases genuinely inside 4 tiles; the 38 beyond it drew walking.
- **`play` needs no gate.** Targeted play sits a median 1 tile from its
  target, max 4 — inside the chase gate anyway. (The code comment claims
  "adjacent by lawfulness"; it is actually 84.4% adjacent with a tail to
  4 tiles. Harmless, but the comment overstates it.)

Biscuit alone is 241 of 418 pounces (58%): `play` 40.7% + `chase` 29.5% =
70.2% of her actions, walking on 3.7% of her ticks. The others run
9.5–15.5% pouncing. Play targets: **bug 97, kitty 52, greeble 18** —
element play outruns kitty play ~2:1, which is F-029 showing up live.

## The gate sweep

`node pose-analyze.mjs census-2026-08-23.jsonl <gate>` (counts below
reproduced independently by a second implementation):

| gate | pouncing | walking | Biscuit | chase ticks demoted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 (was) | 418 · 24.0% | 22.4% | 69.1% | — |
| **3 (shipped)** | **405 · 23.2%** | **23.2%** | **66.8%** | 13, all to walking |
| 2 | 381 · 21.8% | 24.5% | 63.9% | 37, all to walking |

**Why not 2**, though 2 cuts more. Chase distance histogram:
`0:10 1:77 2:48 3:24 4:13 5:9 6:5 …` — it peaks at 1–2 tiles, so a gate
sitting *on* the mode is unstable:

- **Flicker doubles.** Pose flips inside one chase scene go 14 → 30
  (0.33 → 0.70 per scene); scenes flipping 2+ times double from 2 to 4.
  Chase scenes are short (mean 2.21 ticks, median 1), so a quarry drifting
  2↔3 re-poses the cat every tick. Gate 4 works precisely because it sits
  past the mode, out on the tail; 3 still does.
- **It strands lunges.** `node leap-strand.mjs census-2026-08-23.jsonl`:
  6 lunges in the window (all Biscuit) at quarry distances 1,1,1,2,2,3.
  At gate 3 and 4 all six draw as pouncing; **at gate 2 one of six flies
  spec 039's full arc while drawn in a walking pose.** `leapFor` keys on
  the served two-tile step and never reads `pounceGateTiles`, so the gate
  can disagree with a leap but never suppress one. Six events is a thin
  base for the rate; the mechanism is certain.

## ⚠ The drawn/state ~2× is NOT over-drawing

`node scene-vs-action.mjs census-2026-08-23.jsonl`:

| | action ticks | state ticks | ratio | ticks per state run |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| play | 246 | 131 | 1.88× | **1.00** |
| eat | 145 | 73 | 1.99× | **1.00** |
| drink | 161 | 80 | 2.01× | **1.00** |
| groom | 236 | 186 | 1.27× | 2.70 |
| sleep | 351 | 293 | 1.20× | 4.88 |

For play, eat and drink `activity.state` is a **one-tick resolution flag**
— every one of 284 runs was exactly one tick — while `last_action` spans
the whole engagement. The ratio therefore measures action-run length, not
amplification: **eating (1.99×) and drinking (2.01×) both exceed play**,
and nobody claims cats are drawn eating twice as much as they eat.

Reading the action ahead of the state is also the **fix**, settled with
the owner 2026-08-13 (`BACKLOG.md`, "the client draws the wrong pose on
the last tick of every scene"): state-first drew cats standing idle on
17.4% of cat-ticks — drink idle 49.8%, eat idle 50.0% — with the panel's
doing-line contradicting the drawing. Do not revert it to trim pouncing.

The real asymmetry is the **pose**, not the rule: the same rule that
gracefully extends a meal by a tick extends a *pounce* by a tick, and the
pounce is the loudest pose in the vocabulary. That is why 1.88× reads as
excessive and 1.99× does not.
