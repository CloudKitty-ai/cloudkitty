# 037 vs 036's SC-006: what the anchor does when the ceiling moves

Measured 2026-08-18 on `037-camera-zoom-targets`, headless, against a
`--fresh` 5-kitty 20x20 world driven by the shipped server. 1500 contiguous
ticks (500-tick warm-up discarded) = **20 served minutes**, twice the longest
036 sample.

037 is not implemented, so the future ceiling was reproduced through the
**shipped `Camera` class** by setting `nominalAcross = floorTiles` and
`ceilingFactor = ceilingTiles / floorTiles`. `across` is clamped between
`nominalAcross` and their product (`anim.js:2088`), so this is arithmetically
the scheme 037 specifies, run through today's code rather than a model of it.

One `targetFor` per tick, then `anchorId` committed exactly as `update()` does
at `anim.js:2264`. Driving `update()` *and* `targetFor` evaluates the
hysteresis twice against different held state and makes the anchor look
stickier than it is; that was a bug in the first run of this rig.

Positions are the **served** ones (`view = null`), not the eased draw
positions. Same choice for every row, so the comparison is like-for-like.

## Rig validation

036's recorded hysteresis sweep, replayed on its own world, gave **4.20
changes/min at 64% bound** for the shipped 1.5. This rig on an independent
`--fresh` world gives **4.50 at 67%**. Close enough on a different world
instance to trust the method.

## Three findings

**1. 037 cannot change the literal count — only its visibility.** `anchorFor`
never consults the ceiling, so anchor changes/min is identical across every
row. What the ceiling decides is whether a change *moves the aim*
(`anim.js:2134`: `bound && anchor ? at(anchor) : com`). SC-006's literal
reading already fails today at 4.50, which 036 knew and called marginal.

**2. The feature splits the camera in two.** Large viewports get markedly
calmer than today (0.65 visible/min against 2.25); small ones get markedly
more restless (4.50) and fail SC-006 on the visible reading — the one the
owner said she cared about, and the exact invisible-becomes-visible
regression she named on 2026-08-18 when she chose to hold the bar against all
changes.

**3. The clowder does not fit on a small viewport. Ever.**

```
spread (tiles, incl. the 2.6-tile fit margin each side), 5 kitties, 1500 ticks
  min 7.2   p10 12.2   p25 14.2   median 16.2   p75 19.2   p90 21.2   max 24.2
```

A 340px map's 037 ceiling is **6.8 tiles — below the minimum spread ever
observed (7.2)**. The camera is bound 100% of the time: permanently
anchor-driven, permanently letting wanderers leave, never once fitting the
group. To be bound only as often as today a viewport needs a **14.2-tile
ceiling, which at a 50px target is a 710px map**.

**This is US2 failing through the limit US2 does not protect.** FR-005 puts a
minimum on the *floor*, but on a small viewport the binding limit is the
*ceiling*: the camera cannot widen enough to show the meadow, so it shows one
kitty. "A keyhole, not a meadow" is delivered by the ceiling while the floor's
minimum looks after a case that was never the problem.

## Not measured

Anchor changes are counted per tick. The live client recomputes per frame off
eased positions, which can move the anchor between ticks; that would raise
every row equally and is not what distinguishes them.

candidate ceiling that `bound %` barely moves, and `bound %` is the whole of
what the ceiling controls.

## The lever that does work

Where `bound` is ~100% — every viewport under about 640px — **the visible
count IS the literal count**, so SC-006's two readings converge and the only
dial that moves either is the hysteresis. `anchorFor` never reads the ceiling.

## How much to trust the bar

036's sweep on its own world gave **2.40** changes/min at hysteresis 2.0; this
trace gives **3.40** for the same dial. Two independent samples straddle the
3.0 bar. So SC-006 discriminates weakly at 20 minutes, and the argument for
2.5 is margin against that variance rather than a fix for a known failure at
2.0. Both numbers should be judged in motion before either is baked.

## What the table cannot decide

**The differences between 2.5, 3.0 and 4.0 are smaller than the variance
already observed between worlds.** At hysteresis 2.0, 036's trace gave 2.40
changes/min and this one gives 3.40 — a gap of 1.00. The gap from 2.5 to 4.0
is 0.90. So this measurement separates {1.5, 2.0} from {2.5, 3.0, 4.0} and
cannot rank within either group. Picking among the three is an eye judgement
on stickiness, not a number this table supplies.

Two further cautions on the dwell columns:

- They rest on few events — 54 anchor changes at 2.5, 36 at 4.0 over 20
  minutes — so p90 is roughly the fifth-largest of a few dozen values. The
  median is **non-monotonic** (12.8s at 2.5, 11.2s at 3.0), which is that
  noise showing.
- The 174s "longest hold" is the SAME episode in all three rows — one stretch
  where a kitty stayed central — not three independent observations.

## The trade nobody has a criterion for

SC-006 penalises a restless camera and nothing penalises a sluggish one. At
2.5 and above the longest hold more than doubles, and on a viewport where
`bound` is ~100% — anything under about 640px — the frame holds one kitty and
crops the rest. So a phone at hysteresis 4.0 can show a single kitty for
nearly three minutes while the clowder does something else off-screen. That
may be the right answer for a small screen; it is not one SC-006 can see.

## 036 SC-005 is violated at 340px

> **SC-005**: The camera never draws a frame with no kitty in it.

| viewport | fewest ever in frame | empty frames / 1500 |
|---|---:|---:|
| **340px** | **0** | **3** |
| 460px | 1 | 0 |
| 640px | 1 | 0 |
| 1000px | 3 | 0 |
| 1200px | 3 | 0 |

**The target frame is never empty; the easing creates it.** Measured both ways:
0 empty targets, 3 empty eased frames. The anchor logic guarantees a kitty at
the point the camera is heading for, and 036 FR-008 forbids cutting — so the
camera must travel there. Once the frame is ~7 tiles and the gap between two
anchors is wider than that, the trip crosses empty grass. **Two 036 guarantees
that are each correct and jointly broken by a small enough frame.**

## This is not a hysteresis artefact

| hysteresis | 1.5 | 2.0 | 2.5 (shipped) | 3.0 | 4.0 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| empty frames / 1500 | 1 | 0 | 3 | 2 | 1 |

Non-monotonic, and 0–3 events in 1500 ticks. **This sample cannot resolve a
rate**, and nothing here supports blaming or crediting #245. What it does
establish is **existence** — and SC-005 is a "never", so existence is the whole
of the question. The driver is the frame width, not the anchor dial.

An earlier walk of 036's criteria recorded SC-005 as surviving 037 untouched,
