Meadow lab

Every slider writes VIEW.meadow, which is the same object the live renderer reads — so this draws the shipped code, not a copy of it. Cats are here for scale only. Paste the readout into both anim.js and meadow.js to bake.

Occlusion — a cat walking past a shrub

The same cat at five depths relative to one shrub, drawn through the renderer's own sort keys (catSortKey / coverSortKey in meadow.js, shared so this and the live page cannot disagree). The cat should be hidden at −1 and in front at +1; where it flips is bushBase, and how much there is to hide behind is bushLift. Set bushLift to 0 and nothing occludes at all — the shrub no longer leaves its tile.

Ponds — depth, lip, caustics, meniscus

The three shapes this world actually has, drawn through the shipped buildPondLayers and drawPonds. The blur is clamped by each pond’s own inradius: a blur wider than the pond has nothing to be deep in the middle of, and at a flat 0.95 a lone tile reaches 18% depth and even the 2×2 lake only 49%. The number under each shape is what it actually got. The river is here because area-based sizing would call it as roomy as the lake, and it is the blob that most needs a tight blur.

The same render at 3× — a magnifier, not a bigger draw. Every pixel below is a pixel from the strip above, blown up with smoothing off, so the aliasing on a 1.7px meniscus is the aliasing that ships. Drawing at a 3× tile would flatter the art: it would show a 93px tile, and the world’s largest is 54.

All four phases at once, each with a lone tile and the 2×2 lake, since the phase buttons only ever show you one. Night is the one to look at: measured, its water separates from the ground less than half as well as day’s (contrast 1.29 against 1.98), while the shore-to-deep ramp runs more than twice as strong and the meniscus goes from invisible against the ground to a 5.3 rim. The pale ring and dark centre are what “reads as a hole” looks like.

Ground cover — the four styles, same seed

All four bake into the ground cache, which sits under everything: nothing in the renderer y-sorts, so a cat walking across one draws on top of it. Anything that reads as standing up off the ground reads wrong the moment that happens — which is the whole reason cover lies flat. Each panel below draws a cat over a clump so you can judge that directly.

Drifts — the same cover, gathered into places

The left panel is the OLD placement: every tile rolled its own dice, so density looked the same through any window you put over it. The right one is the same world with the fertility field on. The counts are identical — the readout under them says so, and if it ever stops saying so that is a bug, not a taste call. What changes is only where cover lands. If it still looks evenly speckled, raise fertilityCells or the powers.
